The City Does Not
Contain the Life

Eight years of following urban lives across Accra, Johannesburg and Nairobi.
By Kabiri N. Bule · Final ACMS edition · November 2026
Begin with the city
Photograph · Loren Landau

The question

We began by asking what mobility does to urban life: to belonging, relationships, political community and the ways people imagine their futures.

The project changed as it unfolded. Methods shifted. COVID intervened. The cities repeatedly resisted the neat containers we brought to them.

Eight years later, a simpler question remained: can an urban life really be contained by the city in which it is lived?

Belonging without containment

But elsewhere did not mean detached from here.

Across the study samples, most respondents said they belonged in their neighbourhood. Local attachment did not collapse households, relationships, obligations or imagined futures into one place.

58.4% of locally belonging respondents with at least one classifiable future horizon imagined at least one future in a community of origin, elsewhere in the home country, or another country.
Belonging here did not require imagining the whole life here.

About the research · how to read the evidence

Study frame. This visual essay draws on survey, qualitative and spatial work across nine neighbourhoods in Accra, Johannesburg and Nairobi. The raw survey file contains 1,644 records; the descriptive geography used here retains 1,643 records with a valid study city and exactly one valid study neighbourhood. Item-specific denominators vary.

Qualitative archive. The archive contains 110 interview and focus-group transcripts. Quotations are anonymous. Nairobi excerpts use the English translations supplied in the reviewed transcripts. Laughter is shown only where recorded in the transcript.

City edges. Administrative jurisdiction, Africapolis 2020 built-up morphology and a 2015 modelled functional urban area are kept separate because they answer different questions. The functional layer is a harmonised model, not observed commuting.

Life geography. Maps show aggregates rather than individual trajectories. A place appears only when at least five unique respondents are represented; otherwise locations are combined at country level or withheld. Curves indicate relational reach, not literal travel routes.

Future orientation. The 58.4% measure counts locally belonging respondents with at least one classifiable future horizon who located at least one future in a community of origin, elsewhere in the home country or another country. The mapped Future layer is a different object: named destinations that passed spatial and disclosure checks.

Scope. The nine neighbourhoods were purposively selected. The visual essay describes patterns within the study rather than city-wide or continental prevalence.
